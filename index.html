Practicing Hope
For young adults working for change

Hope is something you practice.

Jeff Kisling writes, I sign my email messages “practicing hope”. This page is about where that phrase comes from, and how you can practice it too.

“People often mistake hope for a feeling, but it’s not. It’s a mental discipline, an attentional practice that you can learn.”
“To practice hope is to face hard truths, harder truths than you can face without the practice of hope. You can’t navigate dark places without a light, and hope is that light for humanity’s dark places.”

The rest of the passage, in briefNorton goes on to say this practice takes years. You will fail at it, get better, and fail again. Hope is also different from looking on the bright side, which tends to crack once real life gets heavy. Practiced hope is what lets a person study climate damage, war, genocide, the ways people exploit each other, and even the long, cold future of the universe, without turning away. It helps you see hard things in their larger context and carry joy and grief at the same time, while waiting for peace to come.

Quinn Norton, It Is Bitter Tea That Involves You So: A Sermon on Hope, April 30, 2018. Read the whole sermon there.
